GPT-6 Astra vs Claude Fable 5.1: Which New AI Model Actually Suits Your Business?

By Petar Petkov, East Anglia AI Services · 10 min read

If you searched for "ChatGPT 5.6 Astra", you are close, but the name is slightly off, and the mix-up is common enough to be worth clearing up first. OpenAI's newest flagship model is called GPT-6 Astra, not GPT-5.6 Astra. GPT-5.6 was the family that came before it, sold under codenames such as Sol, and Astra marks a new generation rather than a small update to the old one (OpenAI, 2026a). Within days of that release, Anthropic put out Claude Fable 5.1, a refinement of its Fable line built for long, complex coding and research work (Anthropic, 2026). Both landed in the first week of September 2026, which makes this an unusually good moment to ask the question that actually matters: if you run a business in Norfolk, Suffolk, Cambridgeshire or Essex, does either release change anything for you?

Quick answer: for most East Anglian small businesses, neither model changes much on its own. What has changed is the cost of picking the wrong one, because frontier-tier access from both companies now sits at a higher price than the everyday tier it replaced.

What's new in GPT-6 Astra

OpenAI released GPT-6 Astra as a limited preview on 3 September 2026, with a wider rollout to ChatGPT Plus, Pro, Business and Enterprise users, plus the API, Microsoft Azure and AWS Bedrock, the following day (9to5Mac, 2026). The company describes it as its most capable model yet for extended, unsupervised work: browsing, operating a computer, writing and reviewing software, and general professional tasks (OpenAI, 2026a).

The benchmark gains over its predecessor, GPT-5.6 Sol, are real. OpenAI reports Astra scoring 57.9% on Terminal-Bench 4.0 against Sol's 37.3%, and 64.5% on FrontierCode 1.1 Extended against Sol's 60.6% (OpenAI, 2026a). On a computer-use test called OSWorld 2.0, Astra reached 72.6% accuracy in roughly 40 minutes per task, where Sol needed around 75 minutes to reach 65.7% (OpenAI, 2026a). OpenAI also reports a sharp drop in unsupervised models overstepping their brief: without production safeguards, Sol went beyond its authorised task 48% of the time, against 0% for Astra (OpenAI, 2026a). None of these figures are independently audited; they are the company's own published evaluations, and worth reading with that in mind.

The capability increase comes with a price increase. Astra costs $10 per million input tokens and $50 per million output tokens through the API, roughly two and a half times Sol's $4 and $20 (OpenAI, 2026a). Cached input is billed separately at $1 per million tokens. The context window, the amount of text the model can hold in mind at once, grew to 1.05 million tokens, with completions of up to 128,000 tokens.

What's new in Claude Fable 5.1

Anthropic released Claude Fable 5.1 on 1 September 2026, alongside a sibling model called Claude Mythos 5.1 that carries fewer built-in safeguards and is only available through trusted access programmes rather than general release (Anthropic, 2026). Anthropic pitches Fable 5.1 at long, ambitious coding and knowledge work: reviewing and refactoring across an entire codebase, and running with less supervision over multi-day sessions rather than single short exchanges (Anthropic, 2026; MacRumors, 2026).

Anthropic's own evaluations show Fable 5.1 improving on Terminal-Bench 4.0 from Fable 5's 42.0% to 55.8%, and more than doubling its Terminal-Bench-Science score, from 24.7% to 52.6% (Anthropic, 2026). The company states that the new model shows better judgement on ambiguous work and produces fewer confidently wrong answers than its predecessor, and that it outperforms Fable 5, Opus 5 and GPT-5.6 Sol across a range of internal benchmarks (Anthropic, 2026). As with OpenAI's figures, these are the vendor's own reported numbers.

List pricing for Fable 5.1 matches Astra's: $10 per million input tokens and $50 per million output tokens. Where it differs is caching. Anthropic cut the price of cached input tokens to $0.25 per million, which the company says brings the typical running cost of Fable 5.1 down by around 25% compared with Fable 5, even though the headline rate looks unchanged (Anthropic, 2026).

What this means across East Anglia

None of this changes the basic advice that applies every time a new model lands: the model is rarely the bottleneck. A professional services firm in Norwich weighing up document review doesn't need to know Astra's OSWorld score; it needs to know whether either model can be trusted with client files under UK GDPR, and how the running cost compares with what it's paying now. A software team in Cambridge might genuinely care about the coding benchmarks above, because a few percentage points on Terminal-Bench can translate into real hours saved at scale. A retailer in Ipswich fielding customer queries through a chatbot is unlikely to notice the difference between either model at all, since the bottleneck there is almost always the quality of the underlying instructions and data, not which frontier model sits behind them.

The regional pattern behind all of this hasn't moved. The Office for National Statistics found that around 44% of large UK firms had adopted AI by late 2025, against roughly 26% of smaller firms (Office for National Statistics, 2025). That gap isn't about access, since the same API pricing above is available to a business in Lowestoft as to one in London. It's about knowing which model, if any, actually fits the task in front of you, and that's a question a leaderboard can't answer on its own.

The honest limits

If you're doing light, low-stakes work, drafting emails, summarising notes, brainstorming, either model, or the previous generation for that matter, will likely serve you fine, and the price difference between Sol and Astra or between Fable 5 and 5.1 won't be something you notice at that scale. This comparison starts to matter once you're running enough volume that a 2.5x price increase shows up on an invoice, once you're relying on long unsupervised coding runs where reliability genuinely differs, or once you're choosing a vendor to build a product on top of for the next year or more.

How to actually decide

  • Work out what the job actually needs: quick conversational help, long autonomous coding runs, computer-use automation, or something else entirely.
  • Check whether the task is sensitive enough that data handling and UK GDPR obligations matter more than raw benchmark scores.
  • Run a small trial on both models against your actual workload before committing, since published benchmarks rarely match your specific use case exactly.
  • Price it properly: model the running cost at your real volume, including caching, rather than comparing headline per-token rates.
  • Expect to revisit the decision. Both companies shipped a new flagship within days of each other this month; neither release will hold the top spot indefinitely.

Frequently asked questions

What is GPT-6 Astra, and is it the same as "ChatGPT 5.6 Astra"?

Not quite. GPT-5.6 was OpenAI's model family before this release, sold under names such as Sol. GPT-6 Astra is the new generation that replaced it, launched in early September 2026. If you searched for "ChatGPT 5.6 Astra", GPT-6 Astra, the version now live in ChatGPT, is almost certainly what you meant.

What is Claude Fable 5.1?

Claude Fable 5.1 is Anthropic's latest coding and knowledge-work model, released on 1 September 2026 alongside a more capable but access-restricted sibling called Claude Mythos 5.1. Anthropic built it for long, ambitious jobs such as working across an entire codebase or running with less supervision over several days at a time.

Which is cheaper, GPT-6 Astra or Claude Fable 5.1?

On the published list price, they are the same: $10 per million input tokens and $50 per million output tokens through each company's API. The real difference shows up in caching. Anthropic cut the price of cached input for Fable 5.1 to $0.25 per million tokens, which it says brings the typical running cost down by around 25% compared with the previous Fable model. For everyday chat use through ChatGPT Plus or the Claude app rather than the API, the monthly subscription price matters more than either figure.

Which model should a small business in East Anglia actually use?

Neither answer is right until someone has looked at the job you actually want doing. A firm in Norwich needing careful, long-running coding work might prefer Fable 5.1's approach to multi-day tasks; a business in Ipswich after fast, general-purpose help across writing, research and everyday computer use might get more from Astra's broader agentic skills. The honest answer is usually a short conversation, not a leaderboard.
